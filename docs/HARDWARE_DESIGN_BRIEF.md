# Hardware design brief — WearSeizure-1D accelerator

**For the agent designing our accelerator.** Self-contained: you should be able to
work from this file alone. Where you must open another file, that is a gap — say so.

**Read §1 and §11 before anything else.** §1 says what the design has to *prove*,
not just compute. §11 lists documents in this repository that are stale and will
mislead you if you read them without this file in hand.

---

## 1. What this design has to prove

This accelerator is the **hardware contribution of a paper** targeting IEEE
TBioCAS. It is not a product and not a deliverable to anyone. The software side is
finished: architecture frozen, DFP8 measured at **0.00 pp** of event sensitivity
against FP32 through the real integer datapath, channel ablation complete at three
seeds. What is missing is an accelerator with **measured** numbers.

That makes "it computes the right thing" necessary but nowhere near sufficient. The
project's own go/no-go list says the paper **cannot be submitted** without:

1. **Bit-exact RTL verification** against the golden model — 0 mismatches over
   ≥10,000 windows.
2. **Measured FPGA power**, static and dynamic separated. A Vivado Power Analyzer
   estimate does not satisfy this and never will.
3. **At least one quantified hardware contribution beyond a generic HLS or CPU
   baseline.**

Point 3 is the design problem. Computing a 586k-MAC network at one inference per
second is easy; a competent HLS flow does it. The strongest candidate contribution
is **programmability** (§9): a microcoded datapath that runs a family of 1D-CNN
shapes without resynthesis, where the published FPGA accelerators for this task are
hardwired to one model. Design for that, not just for this network.

### One thing you cannot win, so do not design for it

**You will lose an absolute power comparison against a microcontroller, and that is
a property of the silicon, not of your design.** PL static power on a Zynq-class
part is ~100–200 mW, i.e. 360–720 J/hour. A published MCU comparator runs at 140 µW
average — about 504 mJ/hour. That is a ~1000× gap no RTL can close.

Report instead, and say plainly why:

* **pJ per MAC** — the only device-normalised, cross-platform-fair metric.
* **Dynamic energy per window**, in µJ, separated from static.
* Board power in mW and mJ/hour with the split stated.

## 2. The network

`wearseizure1d_k5only`. Frozen — training recipes change weight *values*, never
shapes. Input **1 channel × 1024 samples**, 4.0 s at 256 Hz, one decision per
second (stride 256 samples).

**Line buffer rule:** `(k − 1) · dilation + 1` samples × `in_channels`.
**Dilation, not kernel size, is what makes a layer expensive.** `context.1.depthwise`
alone is 4,160 B — **61 % of all line-buffer memory** — because k=5 at dilation 16
spans 65 samples of a 32-sample sequence, with 32 samples of padding each side.

### Two traps

**ReLU is on 10 of 15 layers and the pattern is not uniform.** A uniform
ReLU-after-every-conv datapath computes the wrong network, and the output still
looks plausible.

* `stem.0` — yes
* `b1`, `context.0`, `context.1` are depthwise-separable: BN and ReLU come after the
  **pointwise**, so their **depthwise output is NOT activated**
* `b2`, `b3`, `b4` are multi-scale blocks: **both** depthwise and pointwise activated
* `gap`, `fc` — neither

The `relu` flag per layer is in `hardware/export/manifest.json`. Use it; do not infer.

**No skip connections.** Nothing adds its input back, so nothing has to stay live
across a block. If you later support the multi-scale variant (two depthwise branches
concatenated), budget up to a further 4,096 B.

## 3. Three channel configurations

The deployed model reads one channel. Whether the accelerator should support more is
**open — decide it and justify it.** All three are measured, 66 folds × 3 seeds,
same protocol, trained from scratch per fold (no cohort pre-training, no
distillation), so the arms are comparable **to each other only**.

| | **1 channel** | 4 channels | 18 channels |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Event sensitivity | 91.79 % | 93.31 % | **95.20 %** |
| Segment sensitivity | 49.36 % | 59.36 % | **70.51 %** |
| AUROC | 0.8870 | 0.9237 | **0.9586** |
| Accuracy | 98.96 % | 99.10 % | 99.37 % |
| FAR/h | 0.2798 | 0.3334 | 0.2625 |
| Δ event vs 18ch | **−3.41 pp**, CI [−5.83, −0.93] | −1.89 pp, CI [−5.89, **+1.97**] | — |
| Δ segment vs 18ch | −21.16 pp, CI [−26.28, −16.47] | −11.16 pp, CI [−15.18, −7.13] | — |
| Parameters (BN folded) | **11,578** | 11,746 | 12,530 |
| MACs (thop) | **585,920** | 671,936 | 1,073,344 |
| Peak activation | **6.00 KiB** | 8.00 KiB | 22.00 KiB |
| Total on-chip | **18.62 KiB** | 20.79 KiB | 35.55 KiB |
| Input cells needed | 1,024 | 4,096 | 18,432 |

Three things that matter for the decision:

1. **Four channels is not measurably worse than eighteen** — its interval spans
   zero. One channel's does not. The cost is concentrated in the last step down.
2. **Post-processing absorbs 84 % of the deficit.** 21.16 points at segment level
   become 3.41 at event level, because smoothing, hysteresis and run-length
   filtering integrate the score across consecutive windows. That is why a
   single-electrode device works at all, and it is a result worth a figure.
3. **Only the stem changes between arms.** The extra cost is 8 filters × 7 taps ×
   extra channels, and every layer after the stem is byte-identical. A datapath
   parameterised on input channels covers all three with one design.

Do not compare these against the paper's headline **94.95 %** — that configuration
uses cohort pre-training and distillation, which none of these arms do.

## 4. Arithmetic — DFP8

Dynamic fixed point, 8-bit, **per-tensor** power-of-two scales. A tensor stored with
exponent `p` represents the real value `q / 2^p`. There is **no multiplier in the
requantisation path**; the scale is a shift, and the shift is fully determined by:

```
output_shift = p_in + p_weight − p_out
```

**Why DFP8 and not DFP16 or INT8** — measured, 66 folds, through the real integer
datapath including the 48-bit accumulator, shift-based requantisation, saturation,
and the six extra quantisation points between each depthwise/pointwise pair:

| | event sensitivity | FAR/h |
|---|--:|--:|
| FP32 | 0.9848 | 0.2458 |
| **DFP8** | **0.9848** | 0.3086 |
| delta | **0.00 pp**, CI [0.00, 0.00] | +0.0628 |

Sensitivity identical on **all 66 folds**; FAR differs on 13. That combination is
what makes the zero credible: the scores moved, no window moved far enough to flip
a seizure event.

### The requantiser, exactly

1. Accumulate in the accumulator's fixed point (`p_in + p_weight`).
2. **Round to nearest, ties away from zero**, then shift right. Add `2^(shift−1)`
   when the accumulator is non-negative, `2^(shift−1) − 1` when negative.
3. Saturate to `[−2^(n−1), 2^(n−1)−1]` — **asymmetric**: the floor at 8 bits is
   −128, not −127.
4. ReLU, where the layer has one, **after** saturation.

**A truncating shift is wrong.** It disagrees with round-to-nearest on roughly half
of all values by one LSB, and fifteen layers of that does not stay small. Any
document that specifies a plain arithmetic right shift is stale — see §11.

### Biases

Folding BatchNorm into the convolution **creates a per-channel bias** even though
every `nn.Conv1d` in the source model has `bias=False`. The bias is loaded as the
accumulator's **initial value**, so it lives at the accumulator's fixed point
`p_in + p_weight` and is stored at **32 bit**, not at the data width. There are 450.

### Accumulator width

48 bits is what the reference RTL provides. **Measured peak on real data: 19,023 —
15 bits.** The arithmetic ceiling is also low: the widest layer accumulates
64 channels × 5 taps × 127 × 127 = 5.2 × 10⁶, which is 23 bits, and that assumes
every product simultaneously at full scale. **24 bits is sufficient with margin.**
Confirm on your own weights before committing.

### Per-layer quantisation contract

From `hardware/export/manifest.json`. `output_shift` ranges 5–10 and **no two
adjacent layers agree** — a flat value cannot be right for any real network.

| layer | padding | ReLU | p_in | p_weight | p_out | output_shift |
|---|--:|:--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `stem.0` | 3 | yes | 3 | 6 | 3 | 6 |
| `b1.dw` | 2 | **no** | 3 | 8 | 4 | 7 |
| `b1.pw` | 0 | yes | 4 | 5 | 3 | 6 |
| `b2.dw` | 2 | yes | 3 | 5 | 3 | 5 |
| `b2.pw` | 0 | yes | 3 | 6 | 3 | 6 |
| `b3.dw` | 4 | yes | 3 | 6 | 2 | 7 |
| `b3.pw` | 0 | yes | 2 | 6 | 3 | 5 |
| `b4.dw` | 8 | yes | 3 | 5 | 3 | 5 |
| `b4.pw` | 0 | yes | 3 | 6 | 3 | 6 |
| `context.0.dw` | 16 | **no** | 3 | 7 | 4 | 6 |
| `context.0.pw` | 0 | yes | 4 | 6 | 4 | 6 |
| `context.1.dw` | 32 | **no** | 4 | 7 | 4 | 7 |
| `context.1.pw` | 0 | yes | 4 | 5 | 4 | 5 |
| `gap` | 0 | no | 4 | — | 4 | 5 (= log₂ 32) |
| `fc` | 0 | no | 4 | 8 | 2 | 10 |

`input_exponent = 3`. These shifts are for one specific fold's weights
(`chb01__chb01_03`); every fold has its own, so the shift table must be **loadable,
not hardwired**. Note `context.1.dw` needs **padding 32** — a 4-bit padding field
cannot hold it, and `fc`'s shift of 10 exceeds a 6-bit field, though FC may run in
software.

### Two measured facts that close off tempting optimisations

* **Weight SQNR is 34–47 dB; activation SQNR only 22–31 dB.** The activations are
  the quantisation bottleneck. Per-channel *weight* scales attack the wrong term —
  measured at +6.9 dB on the best layer and still not worth a per-channel shift
  table. If you want accuracy back, **16-bit activations with 8-bit weights** is
  the option to price first; weights dominate memory, so it costs little.
* **The final requantisation costs nothing.** Pushing the classifier through the
  same requantiser collapses 254 distinguishable scores into 34, which looks
  alarming, and measures at 0.00 pp of sensitivity and 0.0013 FAR/h over 66 folds.
  Do not spend hardware exposing the raw accumulator.

## 5. Memory

Three different numbers, all correct, answering different questions. Say which one
you mean.

| quantity | value | what it is |
|---|--:|---|
| **Peak adjacent pair** | **6.00 KiB** | the minimum any implementation needs: a layer reads its input while writing its output, so both are live. Peak at `b1.dw` (4,096 in + 2,048 out). **This network has no skip connections, so there is nothing to add.** |
| Fully streaming | 6.7 KiB | all line buffers live at once, no feature maps materialised |
| Layer sequential | 8.1 KiB | per-layer max of `in + out + own line buffer`, peak at `context.1.depthwise` |
| Ping-pong allocated | **8.00 KiB** | what the reference RTL actually reserves: two banks, each sized to the largest map it ever holds, because layers alternate rather than pack |

**Weights and biases:**

| | count | width | bytes |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| weights | 11,128 | 8 bit | 11,128 B = 10.87 KiB |
| biases | 450 | **32 bit** | 1,800 B = 1.76 KiB |
| **total parameters on chip** | | | **12,928 B = 12.63 KiB** |

**Total on-chip = 12.63 + 6.00 = 18.62 KiB** (or 20.62 KiB with the ping-pong
allocation).

> **Do not use the figure "11,786 B of INT8 weights" that appears in several
> documents.** It is wrong in both directions: 11,786 is the *parameter* count
> including BatchNorm affine parameters, which fold away and are never stored; and
> it omits the 32-bit biases that folding creates. The correct figures are above.

Reference RTL's feature-map memory: `FM_BANK_NUM = 16`, `FM_AWIDTH = 10` →
**16 × 1024 = 16,384 cells per buffer**, two buffers. That is what makes 18 channels
infeasible without redesign: 18 × 1024 = 18,432 cells.

**BRAM percentages in this repository are all computed for XC7Z020 and are
obsolete** — the target is now KV260. Quote bytes until you have a KV260 budget.
Note also that what a device consumes is **blocks, not bytes**: a 544-byte buffer
still occupies a whole block, so small buffers belong in LUTRAM.

## 6. Timing and energy

| | |
|---|---|
| Inference rate | **1 per second** (4 s window, 1 s stride) |
| Latency budget | **≤ 2 ms** |
| MACs the accelerator must issue | **489,600** (conv + fc) |
| MACs by `thop` | 585,920 — includes BatchNorm and elementwise ops that **fold away**; this is the figure the paper's gates use, for cross-model consistency |

Both numbers must be stated, with the reason, wherever either appears.

PE sizing from 489,600 MACs and a 2 ms budget = 245 MMAC/s:

| clock | MAC/cycle needed | time per inference | duty cycle |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 MHz | 2.45 → pick **4** | 1.22 ms | **0.12 %** |
| 100 MHz | 8 | 0.61 ms | 0.06 % |
| 50 MHz | 4.9 → pick **8** | 1.22 ms | 0.12 % |

**A 4–8 MAC array suffices. This is not a compute-bound problem.**

### The single most important number in the design

**Duty cycle ~0.12 %.** The accelerator is idle more than 99.8 % of the time, so
**energy per hour is dominated by static power, not by switching**. Consequences:

* Minimising MACs is **not** the primary energy lever.
* **Power-gating the memories between inferences is worth more than shrinking
  36 KiB to 18 KiB.**
* Clock gating, retention strategy and idle leakage decide battery life.

Design the idle path with at least as much care as the compute path. That is also
where a defensible contribution lives.

## 8. Power measurement — the critical path

**Resolve this before writing the first line of RTL.** It has physical lead time and
nothing else in the project does.

The question: **does the KV260 expose a usable on-board power sensor, and at what
granularity?** PYNQ-Z2 does not — in the PYNQ family only ZCU104 does — and the
recorded fallback was an external INA219/INA226 shunt on the 12 V rail, which must
be ordered.

Three options, with the project's own assessment:

* **External shunt** — works anywhere, needs ordering, gives a true board-level
  measurement with static/dynamic separable by clock-gating the design.
* **On-board sensor, if KV260 has one** — verify granularity: a whole-board rail
  that includes the PS tells you much less than a PL rail.
* **Vivado Power Analyzer estimate — violates the go/no-go.** "Measured", not
  "estimated". Do not plan around this.

## 9. Programmability — the candidate contribution

Published FPGA accelerators for this task are hardwired to one model. A microcoded
datapath that runs a *family* of 1D-CNN shapes without resynthesis is the
distinguishing claim available to us, and it is the reason to design a controller
rather than a pipeline.

Envelope to design for, with the margin over what this network actually needs:

| parameter | real max | design for | margin |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| layers (conv + FC) | 17 | **32** | 1.9× |
| channels per layer | 72 | **128** | 1.8× |
| kernel size | 7 | **≤ 7** | — |
| dilation | 16 | **≤ 16** | — |
| taps within one window | 65 | **128** | 2.0× |
| total line buffer | 6.9 KiB | **16 KiB** | 2.3× |
| weight memory (8-bit) | 14.5 KiB | **32 KiB** | 2.2× |
| MACs per window | 650,880 | **1 M** | 1.5× |
| sequence length | 1024 | **≤ 2048** | 2.0× |

Total SRAM 48 KiB — small on any modern part. **The hard part is not fitting. It is
the programmability mechanism**: the instruction or configuration stream, a datapath
parameterised by channel count and dilation, and dynamic line-buffer allocation.

**Five real configurations to prove it on** — these already exist and were trained,
so the claim is demonstrable rather than asserted:

| network | layers | max channels | dilations | MACs |
|---|--:|--:|---|--:|
| `wearseizure1d_k5only` | 14 | 64 | 1,2,4,8,16 | 489,600 |
| `wearseizure1d_k5only_ctx16` | 14 | 48 | 1,2,4,8,16 | 285,216 |
| `wearseizure1d_k5only_wide` | 14 | 72 | 1,2,4,8,16 | 513,568 |
| `wearseizure1d` (multi-scale) | 17 | 64 | 1,2,4,8,16 | 650,880 |
| `wearseizure1d_k3only` | 14 | 64 | 1,8,16 | 480,384 |

Add the 4- and 18-channel arms from §3 and the proof set covers input width too.

A design note worth keeping: the multi-scale variant needs a **shared k3/k5 tap
generator** time-multiplexed on one PE bank, not duplicate per-branch buffers.

## 12. Artefacts you can use today

In this repository, under `hardware/`:

* **`hardware/golden_model.py`** — bit-exact fixed-point reference, **NumPy only,
  no PyTorch**. This is the numerical specification your testbench compares against.
  `python golden_model.py --self-test` checks the arithmetic; `--input` runs a
  window; `--check-overflow` proves the accumulator never wraps.
* **`hardware/export/manifest.json`** — the per-layer contract: shapes, padding,
  dilation, `relu`, `p_in`/`p_weight`/`p_out`, `output_shift`, and per-layer
  calibration statistics.
* **`hardware/export/weights/`** — 26 hex files, `$readmemh`-ready, Unix line
  endings forced (a stray carriage return in a hex file looks like a hardware bug
  for a day).
* **`hardware/export/test_vectors/`** — 16 files: the input window and all 15 layer
  outputs, for layer-by-layer bring-up.

Regenerate any of it with `scripts/export_dfp_hardware.py` (needs the dataset and a
trained checkpoint, both on the GPU servers). Per-layer numbers come from
`scripts/hardware_spec.py`; memory from `scripts/memory_footprint.py`.

The weights in `hardware/export/` are **one fold's** (`chb01__chb01_03`). The
protocol is patient-specific, so there are 66 weight sets and a deployed device
loads its own patient's. Fine for bring-up; not a cohort accuracy figure.

## 13. Open questions — decide these and say why

1. **How many input channels?** §3 has the data. One channel is the deployed
   configuration and the paper's framing; four is not measurably worse than
   eighteen and still fits; eighteen does not fit the reference memory.
2. **New RTL or inherit?** The reference `Configurable_AI_Accelerator-OS` is
   third-party, has PE, line buffer and quantiser, but **no activation unit, no
   dilation support, and a 4-bit padding field that cannot hold the 32 this network
   needs**. Weigh that against writing fresh.
3. **Dataflow.** Output-stationary keeps accumulators in registers and suits a low
   duty cycle. Justify against the alternatives rather than inheriting a choice.
4. **`P_PE` = 4, 8 or 16**, and the clock. §6 says 4 suffices; more buys idle time,
   which at 0.12 % duty cycle may be worth less than the area costs.
5. **Accumulator width.** 48 provided, 15 used, 23 the arithmetic ceiling. 24 bits
   is free area in every PE — confirm and take it.
6. **KV260 resource budget.** Every LUT/DSP/BRAM figure in this repository is for
   XC7Z020. H4's "< 10 %" needs restating in KV260 terms before it means anything.
7. **Where do GAP and FC run?** The reference design puts them on the host ARM,
   which is why it compiles 13 instructions rather than 15. GAP is a sum and a
   shift; FC is 64×2. Both are nearly free in hardware — decide deliberately.
